# Presentation content rules

Read `design-quality-standard.md` first; it is authoritative for presentation design.

1. Give each slide a distinct audience question and a source-backed answer. Preserve necessary detail and qualifications; no universal word cap.
2. Choose a visual grammar that explains the content. Repeated phase labels in identical boxes are not sufficient differentiation.
3. Use a coherent palette with labeled semantic colors, hierarchy, and one primary focal element. Do not substitute decorative icons for information design.
4. Default to neutral sentence-case titles. No automatic CTA, agenda, BLUF, QR appendix, or seven-slide executive cap. Respect the user's requested depth and count.
5. Use readable typography at the actual viewing size. Equal semantic roles have equal font sizes; redesign dense content instead of shrinking essential text.
6. Lists are valid when they communicate parallel items; left-align them and suppress duplicate markers when icons are used.
7. Embed approved local fonts, icons, and images. Inline SVG icons are supported. No remote font or icon CDN; `html-authoring` governs runtime safety.
8. Animation is optional, nonessential, and respects reduced motion. All information must be available in the static final state.
9. Keep customer facts grounded. Separate proposed design, example, estimate, and observed fact. Do not fabricate metrics or guarantee an untested outcome.
10. Label table columns, connectors, ownership boundaries, and status legends. Verify diagrams as information, not just geometry.
11. Size SVGs and text to fit their actual containers. Helper estimates are not substitutes for browser-rendered checks.
12. Review all rendered slides plus a contact sheet, then record structural, editorial, visual, and user-acceptance status separately.

Existing component templates are reusable implementation examples. Adapt their markup to the current theme, content, and safe offline asset policy; do not inherit old marketing language or legacy constraints.
